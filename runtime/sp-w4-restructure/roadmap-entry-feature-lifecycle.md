**[open] Feature lifecycle state — design for cold→warm→repeat-return, not just the cold/demo state.** *(operator ask 2026-06-19)* Features pass through a lifecycle of states as they're used — **cold** (brand-new / empty), **cold→first-session-complete**, **first-return**, and **repeat-return(s)** — and a design that only works in the cold/demo state can degrade badly in the accreted warm/repeat state. Canonical example: a user action that appends to a list looks fine cold, but after many sessions the list grows enormous and the UI/UX breaks down. Every such surface needs a deliberate answer chosen AT DESIGN TIME, not patched after it bloats — pagination/virtualization, collapse/summarize, archive/age-out, relocate it, or cap+overflow. **Fix direction:** make per-feature state-over-time a first-class spec/design concern: (1) a **lifecycle-state checklist** in the PRD/STORIES standards (`_requirements/_standards/*`) — for each feature, name its behavior + UX at cold / first-complete / first-return / repeat-return, and flag any unbounded-growth / accretion surface with its containment strategy; (2) a **design-quality gauntlet axis** — "does this hold up at repeat-return / high-volume state?" (the gauntlet today reviews the rendered cold state). Finer-grained than, and complementary to, the product-phase lifecycle model (Research→Early-Dev→Launch→Finding-PMF→PMF) — that's the product's arc; this is each feature's state-over-time. *(DISCUSSED-2026-06-19)*
