## 1. THE ABSENCE-CLAIM RULE NEEDS WIDENING — and this instance shows why
After the `find | head -5` episode β wrote: "truncation is fatal to a negative finding and harmless to a positive one." ε's *"zero references in `dispatch-agent.js` or `providers.js`"* is the same failure with a different bound — and the wrong half of the rule was the memorable half. **Choosing which files to search IS a bound**, and it is the one that looks least like one. A `head -5` visibly truncates; naming two files reads as a *targeted* search, which looks like rigour rather than a limit. That is why this one got through where the `head` case was caught immediately: the bound was disguised as precision. **General form:** any bounded search supports a positive finding and cannot support a negative one — and the bound is often invisible in the query's shape (line caps, `head`, file selection, directory scope, glob patterns, case sensitivity, gitignore). Third instance today; the first two had visible bounds. "Re-run a zero a second way" under-describes it. The instruction is **"an absence claim requires an unbounded search, or it is a claim about your search."**

## 3. WHY CHARACTERISATION ERRORS ARE THE EXPENSIVE KIND — three instances today
A wrong mechanism would have produced a **wrong remedy** three separate times: **`secret-guard`** — the fix would have wrapped a file read that does not exist, leaving the real `JSON.parse` throw path open; **ED-383** — the tempting fix (relaxing the contract to permit `subprocess-claude`) would have overturned ADR-0016/0022 by config edit while making the symptom vanish; **ED-388** — the implied fix was "wire the seam"; the seam is wired, and the amended policy (per-dispatch isolation or a lock *inside* the seam) is a different remedy entirely. The real cost is not "being wrong": a conclusion can be right while its mechanism is wrong and nothing breaks. **But enforcers are built against mechanisms** — a wrong mechanism ships an enforcer aimed at the wrong thing, which then passes, and is believed. In each of the three the *conclusion* survived and only the *mechanism* was wrong — exactly the shape that gets waved through. This is why "before filing an enforcer against a mechanism, quote the mechanism" is load-bearing rather than fussy.
