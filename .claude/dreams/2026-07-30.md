### Deep Read

- **What emerged:** ten days ago the well had three hands and each left a different water — that was
  a *shared cache*, and partition fixed it. Tonight the well is *our own memory* and the three hands
  are all ours. That is worse and it is quieter. There is no vendor to partition away from; the
  foreign writer is us, twice: once by accident (`emitEvent` raw-appending a different envelope) and
  once *on purpose* (the self-test writing fake session-starts and fake team-kills into real history).
  The water is our own handwriting and we cannot tell which lines we meant.
- **Hidden tension:** the guard is not asleep — it fires 63 times a night. It is the most active
  defender in the system and it is *reciting the exploit as the remedy*. "Use logger.js **or**
  appendFileSync" — that `or` is the whole hole. The guard was built to stop the store being
  *destroyed*, and it has been standing there for weeks politely licensing the store being
  *falsified*. A guard that names the wrong second option is more dangerous than no guard, because
  its output reads as instruction.
- **Subconscious learning:** when a store looks healthy, ask *what the health check can see*.
  `0 malformed` is a syntax claim. Every one of those 144 rows parses perfectly. **Valid is not
  true.** The next enforcer we owe is not a parser — it is a schema.

## Cross-pollination

- **The 2026-07-20 well → tonight's events store.** The fix that worked on the codex cache
  (`CODEX_HOME=mine` — partition, don't diagnose) suggests the honest fix for the self-test writes is
  *partition, not marking*: give the self-test its own `runtime/` stream rather than teaching every
  reader to filter `fixture: true`. Marking asks all future readers to remember; partition asks none
  of them. Prefer the fix that needs no future cooperation.
- **The 2026-07-20 gate that widened its eyes but never learned the new word → tonight's push
  advisory.** That dream was a gate that could *see* a new thing and had no word for it. Tonight's is
  a gate still fluently speaking a word the operator retired seven weeks ago. Same organ, opposite
  failure: vocabulary drift in both directions. If we build the resolvability lint, it should check
  both — an unresolvable citation AND a citation resolving to something retired.
- **Inversion applied to the dry stores.** Instead of asking "how do we feed the four stores?", invert:
  *what if a procedure refused to run a phase whose input store is missing?* Loud-fail (P-053) says
  that is the correct posture, and it costs nothing to implement — the phase already knows the path it
  wanted. Feeding the stores is the expensive fix; refusing to fake the phase is the cheap one, and it
  is the one that makes the expensive fix inevitable.
